## Design Context

### Users
- **Storyteller**: runs a game night from a laptop or tablet, sometimes casting the Run screen to a TV for the table. Jobs: read scenes aloud with good pacing, run the mad-lib character creation, pick outcomes, keep track of HP/items/levels without breaking immersion. Before the session they also write stories in the Editor, usually on a desktop.
- **Players**: friends at the same table, on their own phones, in a dim room, glancing between the storyteller and the screen. They can't act on anything; their screen is ambient: who we are, how hurt we are, what we're choosing between.

### Brand Personality
Cinematic, theatrical, immersive. The app should feel like the house lights going down: the interface gets out of the way and the story takes the stage. Emotional goals: anticipation (choices, the d20 roll), dread when HP is low, a real payoff at victory or defeat.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **App shell (Library, Editor, Run)**: dark, stage-like, scene-first. Chrome recedes (low-contrast controls, revealed on hover or focus). The scene text is the hero, set in a book-quality serif at reading size with generous measure.
- **Story themes go deep**: each of the six themes (cyberpunk, fantasy, deep_space, modern, horror, insanity) owns its display font, palette, background texture, custom SVG health icon, motion personality, and its victory/defeat moment. The themes differ in more than colour.
- **Light/dark**: the global toggle stays (project rule). Every theme must define both modes deliberately and pass contrast checks in both.
- **Presentation mode**: the storyteller can switch the Run screen into a projected or shared view that hides spoilers (madlib template, `{key}` hints, choice outcomes) and all controls except the essentials.
- **Anti-references**: generic SaaS dashboards, indigo-on-charcoal defaults, emoji as iconography, pill-button rows of equal weight, left-border accent cards, centred everything.

### Design Principles
1. **The story is the stage.** Scene text and the current moment always win the hierarchy. Everything else is lighting and props.
2. **Themes are worlds, not skins.** If swapping a theme changes only colours and a font, it isn't done.
3. **No spoilers on shared glass.** Anything players might see (phones, a TV) never shows outcomes, templates, or hidden mechanics.
4. **Build to the peaks.** Spend the motion and polish budget on choice reveals, HP loss, the d20 finale, and the outcome.
5. **Players never know where they are.** No scene counts or progress on any player-facing screen (phones, TV presentation mode). Only the storyteller's private view shows progress. The finale is revealed dramatically, as the one moment players learn it's the end.
6. **Glanceable at arm's length.** The player view is read in a dark room, mid-conversation: big type, clear state, no clutter.
